# Training principles and supporting guidance

Use reward-based training, environmental management, choice and gradual exposure below distress thresholds. Identify function and emotion. Do not use intimidation, flooding, leash jerks, painful equipment or dominance corrections. A timer guides the person; it never requires the dog to endure stress.

These are educational exercises based on professional welfare guidance, not independently validated branded protocols. The sources are guidance pages, not invented trials. No cure rates or comparative superiority claims are implied. Outbound requests to these sites returned proxy 403 responses in this environment; live page availability and current wording were not independently rechecked here.

## Approach mapping and evidence limits

| Lessons                                              | Basis and limits                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Calm at Home, Doorbell Detective, Strangers Are Okay | ASPCA Barking + AVSAB. Identify triggers, reduce exposure, pair comfortable cues with rewards and reinforce alternatives. Check needs for attention barking. Distance work is not bite-risk assessment.   |
| Tiny Goodbyes                                        | ASPCA Separation Anxiety + AVSAB. Below-threshold absences with camera observation. Individual thresholds may be fractions of a second; the timer is never a target. Severe cases need a veterinary plan. |
| Everyday Departures                                  | ASPCA Separation Anxiety. Comfortable departure-cue practice complements gradual absence work. It does not prove alone-time tolerance.                                                                    |
| My Cozy Spot                                         | ASPCA Separation Anxiety + AVSAB. Voluntary relaxation with the owner present. A resting spot does not treat separation anxiety by itself; confinement can worsen distress.                               |
| Chew This Instead, Trade It!, Puppy-Proof Paradise   | ASPCA Destructive Chewing + AVSAB. Management, safe chewing and voluntary exchanges. Trade only low-value, non-guarded objects; guarding requires qualified support.                                      |
| Loose Leash Magic, Walk With Me, Sniff & Stroll      | RSPCA training + AVSAB. Reinforce slack lead, attention and comfortable exploration. Specific sniff/walk cue arrangements are practical synthesis, not separately validated clinical protocols.           |
| Happy Bathroom, Splash Confidence, Fresh & Fluffy    | Dogs Trust grooming + AVSAB. Gradual exposure, non-slip footing, choice, handling breaks and appropriate products. Individual tolerances and bath frequency vary.                                         |

Approaches serve different functions and can complement each other. They are not ranked as equally effective treatments. Relaxation or cue practice is not a replacement for individualized separation treatment. Diagnosis and medication belong with a vet.

## Safety

Pause for freezing, escape attempts, stiff posture, tucked tail, repeated lip licking or refusal of a normally desired treat. Read the whole dog and context; no one signal diagnoses emotion. Offer space/rest and reduce difficulty later. Never recommend cry-it-out or repeated overwhelming absences.

Persistent/severe distress, sudden behaviour changes, pain or bite risk need qualified veterinary behaviour support. Never leave a dog unattended in a vehicle where temperature or other hazards may arise. Non-food ingestion can cause obstruction: suspected ingestion, vomiting, abdominal pain or lethargy needs prompt veterinary advice. Avoid chasing, forced removal, splintering chews and tooth-breaking objects. Persistent odour, skin irritation or coat changes warrant a vet; unnecessary frequent bathing is not a goal.

## Product progress and tips

The three-milestone/six-day rule is an explicit product heuristic, not a clinically validated recovery measure. It relies on reported observations, not automated verification. A completed foundation begins optional maintenance and does not guarantee permanent resolution. Tips are practical educational synthesis linked to professional guidance families, not quotations or claims from individual studies. Dog comfort and professional advice take precedence.
