# Training principles and supporting guidance

Use reward-based training, environmental management, choice and gradual exposure below distress thresholds. Identify function and emotion. Do not use intimidation, flooding, leash jerks, painful equipment or dominance corrections. A timer guides the person; it never requires the dog to endure stress.

These are educational exercises based on professional welfare guidance, not independently validated branded protocols. The sources are guidance pages, not invented trials. No cure rates or comparative superiority claims are implied. Outbound requests to these sites returned proxy 403 responses in this environment; live page availability and current wording were not independently rechecked here.

Approaches serve different functions and can complement each other. They are not ranked as equally effective treatments. Relaxation or cue practice is not a replacement for individualized separation treatment. Diagnosis and medication belong with a vet.

## Safety

Pause for freezing, escape attempts, stiff posture, tucked tail, repeated lip licking or refusal of a normally desired treat. Read the whole dog and context; no one signal diagnoses emotion. Offer space/rest and reduce difficulty later. Never recommend cry-it-out or repeated overwhelming absences.

Persistent/severe distress, sudden behaviour changes, pain or bite risk need qualified veterinary behaviour support. Never leave a dog unattended in a vehicle where temperature or other hazards may arise. Non-food ingestion can cause obstruction: suspected ingestion, vomiting, abdominal pain or lethargy needs prompt veterinary advice. Avoid chasing, forced removal, splintering chews and tooth-breaking objects. Persistent odour, skin irritation or coat changes warrant a vet; unnecessary frequent bathing is not a goal.

## Product progress and tips

The three-milestone/six-day rule applies to practice records across all lesson levels. It is an explicit product heuristic, not a clinically validated recovery or skill-reliability measure. It relies on reported observations, not automated verification. A completed practice record begins optional maintenance and does not guarantee permanent resolution or readiness for the next difficulty. Prerequisite links and behavioral readiness are educational guidance; all lessons remain browsable. Automatic recommendations use prerequisite completion to avoid unexpectedly moving a new dog straight to an advanced exercise, but the dog's current comfort takes precedence.
